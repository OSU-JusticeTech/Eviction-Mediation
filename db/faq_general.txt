Q: What is the difference between negotiation or mediation? 
A: 
**Negotiation** is when you and the other person talk directly to try to solve a disagreement. In this tool, landlords and tenants begin by negotiating through a text-based chat. You can explain your concerns, ask questions, and discuss possible solutions.
 
**Mediation** is when a neutral person called a mediator joins the conversation to help both sides communicate and work toward an agreement. The mediator does not take sides or make decisions for you.
 
You can reach an agreement through negotiation without ever needing a mediator.
 
Q: What is Mediation? 
A: 
Mediation is a way to work through a disagreement with help from a neutral person called a **mediator.** The mediator listens to both sides and helps them talk about the problem and possible solutions. 
 
The mediator does not take sides, decide who is right or wrong, or force anyone to agree. The mediator is also not your lawyer and cannot give legal advice. However, they may direct you to other resources if you need legal information or help. 
 
You and the other person decide whether you can reach an agreement. 
 
Q: When can I use this tool?
A:
You can use this tool to work through landlord-tenant problems **before an eviction is filed.** This may include:
	- Unpaid rent
	- Repairs or maintenance
	- Lease questions
	- Communication problems
	- Other landlord-tenant disagreements
Mediation may also be available **after an eviction is filed but before the court hearing.**
 
 
Q: Why should I try negotiation and mediation? 
A: 
 
Negotiation and mediation give landlords and tenants a chance to solve problems together. They may help tenants avoid eviction and landlords avoid finding a new tenant. Going to court can take time, cost money, and cause stress. In court, a judge may decide the outcome. Through negotiation or mediation, you and the other person decide what works for you.  
 
Negotiation and mediation may help both sides avoid some of the costs of an eviction.
 
**Possible Costs for Landlords** 
	- **Total cost:** $5,000+ 
	- **Court filing fees:** $128–$165
	- **Attorney fees:** About $150
	- **Writ of Restitution (Red Tag):** $35
	- **Set-out costs:** $45
	- **Cleaning or repairs:** About $3,000
	- **Lost rent:** varies
	- **Missed work:** Time away from work to attend court
	- **Time:** An eviction may take several weeks or longer
 
**Possible Costs for Tenants**
	- **Eviction record:** An eviction filing may appear in court records and may affect future housing applications.
	- **Past-due rent:** You may still owe unpaid rent, property damage costs, or interest.
	- **Transportation:** Parking, bus fare, or other costs to get to court.
	- **Missed work:** You may lose income from taking time off for court.
	- **Time:** Traveling to court and attending hearings takes time.
	- **Stress:** Going through an eviction case can be stressful.
 
Mediation may help landlords and tenants avoid some of these costs by working toward a solution before the dispute goes further.  
 
Q: Does everyone have to agree to participate?
A: 
Yes. Both the landlord and tenant must agree to participate. Negotiation and mediation are **voluntary**. Both the landlord and tenant must agree to participate. If the other person does not want to use the tool, you cannot force them to join. 
 
You, the other person, or the mediator may end mediation at any time. Neither person can be forced to reach an agreement. 
 
Q: What happens when we reach an agreement?
A:
You and the other person decide what you agree to. You can use the agreement templates in the **Generate Document** tab to put your agreement in writing, so everyone understands what they agreed to do. 
 
Both people can review the terms, suggest changes, and accept, reject, or make a counteroffer before signing. A mediator can help with this process if one has been invited to join the conversation. 
 
The agreement is not automatically filed with the court. However, if someone does not follow through with their end of the agreement, the other person may be able to take legal action for breaking it. 
 
Both people are expected to follow the agreement, so only agree to something you understand and believe you can do.  
 
Q: What if we cannot reach an agreement?
A:  
Negotiation or mediation does not always end in an agreement. Either person can decide to stop participating.
